# Video rubric

The video verifier reads this file on every call. Edit it freely on the day.

The clip is animated from the approved still, so composition, taste and brief fit are already settled
by `rubric.md`. This file is only about what moving adds, and what moving breaks.

## Motion (what makes someone keep watching)

- **First-second event.** Something visibly starts within the first second. A clip that sits still and then moves has already been scrolled past.
- **The scene changes, not just the camera.** A slow push-in on a frozen scene is a still with extra steps. Someone or something in the frame has to do something.
- **It is the concept's moment.** The movement plays out the moment the concept describes, or its direct consequence. Hair, steam and a drifting camera do not count.
- **One continuous shot.** No cuts and no jumps to another angle or scene.
- **Believable physics.** Liquids fall, objects keep their weight and shape, nothing passes through anything else, and nothing arrives from nowhere.

## Motion slop flags (any one blocks shipping; list each one you see)

- Frozen: nothing in the scene moves.
- Dead first second: no visible event has started by one second in.
- Only the camera moves: a pan, zoom or drift over a scene where nothing happens.
- Unintended cut: the shot jumps to a different framing, angle or scene.
- Pop or flicker: something appears, vanishes or changes within one or two frames.
- Morphing: a hand, face, object or piece of text melts, merges or changes shape as it moves.
- Identity drift: by the end, the person, object, text or setting is no longer the one the clip started with.
- Impossible motion: something moves in a way real physics does not allow.
- Motion misses the idea: what moves has nothing to do with the concept's moment.

## Scoring guide

Score the motion criteria above. Do not lower the score because of slop flags: report them instead.
The harness counts them separately.

- **9-10:** you would post this as is; the movement is the reason to watch.
- **7-8:** a clear event from the first second that delivers the concept.
- **5-6:** it moves, but the movement adds little to the still.
- **3-4:** barely moves, or moves in a way that has nothing to do with the idea.
- **0-2:** broken.
